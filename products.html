OXIQEL ONE: home console

A compact living room console built on a catalogue AMD APU, the Ryzen 7 8700G class, on a compact board from a manufacturing partner. The honest positioning matters to us: there is no console class custom chip a company at our volume can buy, so ONE is designed and described as a compact living room PC for indie, back catalogue, esports class and cloud gaming, never as a rival to a subsidised flagship console.

Design target: primary mode
1080p play, with upscaled output above it. Target only, not achieved.
Memory and storage
16 GB DDR5 in the base design, 32 GB recommended above it. Storage 1 TB to 4 TB on a user replaceable M.2 NVMe slot.
Connections
HDMI 2.1 output, USB A and USB C, Gigabit Ethernet or better, Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth 5 class for controllers.
Disc drive
None in generation one. A USB external drive can be an accessory.

OXIQEL GO: handheld

An OLED handheld designed around the AMD Ryzen Z series, with the flagship design on the Z2 Extreme class part and a base design on the Z1 Extreme class, supply permitting and confirmed in writing. GO docks over USB C to a television and carries the same account and library as ONE. Battery expectations are set by arithmetic, not adjectives: a handheld in this power class runs demanding titles for roughly one and a half to two and a half hours, and lighter titles longer. No all day gaming claim will ever appear here.

Design target: display
8.0 to 8.8 inch OLED, 1920 by 1200, 120 Hz, variable refresh. Target only, not achieved.
Memory and storage
16 GB LPDDR5X, 32 GB on the premium design. M.2 2230 NVMe 512 GB to 2 TB, microSD expansion.
Controls
Integrated Hall effect sticks and triggers, six axis motion sensing.
Power profiles
Designed around 10 W battery, 17 W balanced and 25 W plugged profiles, with a higher docked profile described as a docked profile, not a second product.

OXIQEL CAST: cloud device

CAST renders nothing locally except video decode and the interface. The game runs somewhere else: first on your own ONE or PC on your home network, the lowest latency and lowest cost mode, and on a cloud service when that service exists. The box variant is the recommended design because it allows a proper heatsink, wired Ethernet and USB for controllers. CAST generation one is a streaming client for the OXIQEL ecosystem, not a general smart television platform, and it claims no streaming service certification it does not hold.

Design target: decode
Hardware AV1, H.265 and H.264 decode to 4K60 with HDR10. Target only, not achieved.
Box design
4 GB memory, 32 GB storage, Gigabit Ethernet, Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5 class.
Latency
Design budget: CAST itself adds under 10 ms of decode and display latency at 1080p60. Total cloud latency depends on network distance and is never marketed as a single guaranteed number.

OXIQEL CONTROL: controller

CONTROL starts from an established manufacturing platform with a proven radio and board, and differentiates where it is cheap and visible: shell design, grip texture, button feel, back button layout and firmware. The differentiator is the stick modules: Hall effect and TMR sensing, which read stick position magnetically and have no potentiometer contact to wear, the mechanism behind stick drift. Replacement Hall modules are a published retail product category, and the manufacturing shortlist for CONTROL is built around the suppliers of that platform.

Sticks and triggers
Hall effect or TMR sticks, Hall effect analogue triggers, remappable back buttons, six axis motion sensing.
Connections
Bluetooth LE, a 2.4 GHz dongle mode and USB C wired, with pairing for three hosts.
Software
Full remapping, dead zone curves and trigger settings in the configuration app. Adjustable dead zones are also an accessibility feature.
Deliberately not promised
Adaptive triggers and voice coil haptics are not in the baseline design. That mechanism sits in an active patent area and needs intellectual property review before any imitation.

OXIQEL VISION AIR: display glasses

VISION AIR is a wearable monitor. It is not a VR headset, it has no positional tracking, and it must never be described as virtual reality or spatial computing. The image sits in front of your eyes and moves with your head, exactly like the leading products in its category, which carry no battery, no processor and no operating system of their own: they are powered and fed video by the host over USB C. VISION AIR is the natural companion to GO: a private big screen for a handheld.

Design target: panels
Micro OLED, 1920 by 1080 per eye, 120 Hz, birdbath optics, 50 degree field of view target. Target only, not achieved.
Host connection
USB C DisplayPort Alt Mode video and power. Hosts without DisplayPort Alt Mode output will not work, and that fact will be printed on the box.
Fit
Design target under 75 g, adjustable nose pads and temples, a prescription insert program or built in diopter adjustment.
Tracking
None. Zero degrees of freedom. An optional software screen pinning feature may be validated later and would still not make this a VR product.

OXIQEL VISION: VR headset

A standalone VR and MR headset planned on the Qualcomm XR2 Gen 2 class reference design ecosystem, the same published route the industry uses to bring headsets to market without reinventing tracking. VISION is sequenced last in the line on purpose: a headset with no content ecosystem is the hardest product in the catalogue to support, so it launches after the account, the store and the other devices exist. Camera, passthrough and hand tracking data are privacy sensitive by design: on device processing by default, explicit permission prompts, and a recording indicator that applications cannot disable.

Design target: platform
XR2 Gen 2 class processor, 8 GB memory, 128 GB storage in the base design, with a wired and wireless PC link mode as a feature.
Design target: displays
Dual LCD at 2064 by 2208 per eye class, pancake optics, 90 Hz default with a 120 Hz experimental mode. Targets only, not achieved.
Tracking
Inside out six degree of freedom tracking, controller and hand tracking, full colour passthrough for mixed reality.
Application standard
The OpenXR application interface is the portability commitment. No conformance branding is used until the formal adopter and testing process is complete.
